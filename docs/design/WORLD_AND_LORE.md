# World and lore

## The setting: Namarra Basin

[PROPOSED] Namarra Basin is a ring of warm inland waters, uplifted forest, open grazing shelves, and storm-cut coast. The land is old, but its living population is in a recurring phase of abundance. Players are **returning lineages**: each new body carries inherited tendencies and memories as story context, not a human soul or a chosen-one plot.

### Light mystery

The Basin’s seasonal pulse repeatedly wakes dormant egg beds and reshapes migration routes. No human explanation is required. The mystery is ecological and optional: players can compare nesting marks, mineral rings, and animal behavior, but survival remains the main story. The setting should support theories without requiring a lore questline or a speaking narrator.

In setting terms, dinosaurs persist because the Basin’s dormant egg beds are part of that recurring ecological pulse; no human intervention is needed to justify their return.

## Biomes and gameplay purpose

| Biome | Purpose and social pressure |
| --- | --- |
| **Sunken Reedwater** | Shallow water, fish, reeds, and concealment; a useful meeting point with poor visibility and ambush risk. |
| **Redglass Shelf** | Open mineral flats and long sightlines; migration, display, and group defense are visible from far away. |
| **Morrowwood** | Dense canopy, fruiting routes, echoes, and broken sightlines; favors scouts, small bodies, and careful sound use. |
| **Ashstep Ravines** | Dry cuts and thermal vents; short routes, scarce water, and hazard-driven crowding. |
| **Tidefall Coast** | Storm pools, nesting ledges, and stranded carcasses; tides change access and expose routes. |

## Landmarks and meeting points

- The **Returning Stones**: a ring of mineral pillars where lineages leave readable, consent-controlled memorial marks.
- The **Longwater Crossing**: a shallow crossing with multiple approaches, visible from the shelf, suitable for planned reunions and dangerous enough to create stories.
- The **Hollow Nestworks**: a protected basin of old egg beds; nesting is valuable, public, and never a guaranteed safe zone.
- The **Thunder Arch**: a storm-cut overhang that provides a recognizable fallback route, but can trap a group when weather turns.

Landmarks are navigation aids and social choices, not guaranteed safe hubs. Their exact layouts and event rates remain [PROPOSED].

## Environmental hazards

- Flash floods and tide surges close familiar routes and can separate a party.
- Thermal vents damage health or force rerouting; warning sound and visible heat must precede danger.
- Falling stone, mud shelves, and unstable crossings punish careless sprinting without becoming random instant death.
- Heat, cold rain, smoke, and darkness change stamina recovery, scent travel, and visibility.
- Resource bloom and scarcity move pressure between biomes instead of pinning every player to one optimal location.

## Day, night, and seasons

[PROPOSED] A day/night cycle changes visibility, calls, predator confidence, and resting risk. [PROPOSED] Seasonal phases change plant abundance, water reliability, migration, nesting windows, and carcass persistence. These are ecosystem drivers, not mandatory calendars: a friend session should still have a useful plan if it starts near a transition.

Weather must be readable before it is dangerous. A season may change the safest route, but it must not invalidate a role or require a full-time schedule. Region instances can be in different phases only if the service clearly communicates the phase on join.

## Tone guidelines

- Wonder is quiet and physical: tracks, calls, weather, and a remembered route beat exposition.
- Danger is tense, not sadistic; losses have causes players can understand.
- Friendship is shown through escorting, warning, sharing a carcass, guarding a nest, and choosing when not to fight.
- No human military, corporation, time-travel exposition, or mandatory speech is needed to explain the world.
- Avoid joke skins, contemporary signage, and spectacle that makes the ecosystem feel disposable.

## IP-safety checklist

- Use original world, biome, landmark, archetype, naming, silhouette, color language, sound direction, and lore.
- Do not copy names, maps, lore, quest text, UI, code, assets, server rules, or story content from The Isle, Path of Titans, or Beasts of Bermuda.
- Use those games only for high-level genre patterns, with sources recorded in docs/design/GAME_VISION.md.
- Review each species concept for accidental resemblance to a branded species design; change the concept when resemblance is not necessary.
- Keep references and inspirations separate from implementation files; do not import third-party assets or code without verified rights.
